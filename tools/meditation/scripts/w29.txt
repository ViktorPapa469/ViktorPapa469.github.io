title: Anger as a Messenger
aspect: Understanding anger
line: Anger often points to something we care about.
invite: When anger rises this week, pause for three breaths before you speak or act, and ask: what does this anger want me to know?
===
This week we look at anger. Many of us were taught that anger is bad, and that we should hide it. Others find it bursts out before we can stop it.

But anger is not the enemy. It is a messenger. It often tells us that a boundary has been crossed, or that something we value is threatened. The problem is not feeling angry. The problem is acting on anger before we have listened to its message.
[pause 15]
[anchor]
Let's begin by grounding ourselves. Feel the weight of your body. Feel the steadiness of the breath. From here, we can look at strong feelings without being swept away.
[pause 60]
Now think of a recent time you felt irritated or angry. Not the most intense time. Something mild to moderate. Remember the situation.

Notice what happens in the body as you remember. Heat in the face, perhaps. Tension in the jaw or the fists. A quickening of the heart.
[pause 90]
Breathe with these sensations. Let them be there, without feeding them with more thoughts, and without pushing them away. Just feel the energy of anger in the body.
[pause 90]
Now ask the anger, gently: What are you trying to tell me? What do you want to protect? Listen for the answer. Perhaps it is a need for respect. For fairness. For being heard. For safety.
[pause 120]
Notice that underneath the anger, there is often something softer. Hurt, perhaps. Or fear. Or a deep care for something. See if you can touch that softer feeling with kindness.
[pause 120]
Breathing in, I feel the heat. Breathing out, I listen to its message.
[pause 60]
This week, here is your invitation. When anger rises, pause for three breaths before you speak or act. Ask: What does this anger want me to know? Then respond from that understanding.
[pause 20]
