title: Restlessness
aspect: Staying with discomfort
line: The urge to move on is just another feeling. You can let it pass.
invite: When you feel the urge to reach for your phone this week, wait for three breaths first, and notice what the urge feels like.
===
This week we sit with restlessness and boredom. These feelings are familiar to anyone who has tried to meditate, or to focus. The body wants to move. The mind wants something new. Anything but this.

In a world full of instant distraction, we rarely let these feelings pass on their own. We reach for the phone the moment we feel a flicker of boredom. This week, we practise staying a little longer. And discovering what lies on the other side.
[pause 15]
[anchor]
As you sit here, notice any restlessness. Perhaps an itch, an urge to shift position, a thought saying: How much longer?

Don't act on it just yet. Just notice it with interest.
[pause 75]
Where is the restlessness in the body? Perhaps in the legs, the hands, the chest. What does it actually feel like? Buzzing? Pressure? A pulling?
[pause 90]
Notice that restlessness rises and falls, like a wave. If you wait, it peaks, and then it begins to fade on its own.
[pause 90]
Now notice boredom, if it is here. The flat, grey feeling of nothing interesting happening. Look at it closely. What is boredom made of? Often, if we look carefully, it dissolves into simple sensations, and a gentle kind of space.
[pause 120]
On the other side of boredom, there is often something quieter. Rest. Creativity. A deeper kind of attention. You can only find it by staying.
[pause 120]
Breathing in, I notice the urge. Breathing out, I stay.
[pause 60]
This week, here is your invitation. When you feel the urge to reach for your phone, wait for three breaths first. Notice what the urge feels like. Then decide freely, instead of automatically.
[pause 20]
[closing]
