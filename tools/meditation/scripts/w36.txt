title: Honouring Limits
aspect: Tiredness and care
line: Your limits are not failures. They are information.
invite: This week, when you feel tired, ask your body what it needs, and give it at least one small thing.
===
This week we look at tiredness and limits. We often treat tiredness as weakness, something to push through with coffee or willpower. We feel we should be able to do more, always.

But the body has limits for a reason. Tiredness is a message, asking for rest, food, movement, or simply a change of pace. When we listen to it, we last longer, and work better. This week, we practise honouring our limits with kindness.
[pause 15]
[anchor]
Let's begin by checking in. On a scale from empty to full, how much energy do you have right now? There's no right answer. Just notice honestly.
[pause 60]
If there is tiredness, where do you feel it? In the eyes, perhaps. The shoulders. The back. A heaviness in the whole body. Breathe gently into those places.
[pause 90]
Notice any thoughts that come with the tiredness. Perhaps: I shouldn't be tired. I haven't done enough. See if you can let those thoughts soften. Tiredness is not a moral failing. It's simply how a body feels after giving.
[pause 90]
Imagine your energy as water in a well. Each day you draw from it. Rest, food, sleep, joy and kindness are the rain that fills it again. What has been filling your well lately? What has been draining it?
[pause 120]
Now ask yourself gently: What is one thing I could do this week to refill my well, even a little?
[pause 120]
Breathing in, I notice my limits. Breathing out, I care for myself within them.
[pause 60]
This week, here is your invitation. When you feel tired, ask your body what it needs. Water, rest, a stretch, fresh air, an early night. And give it at least one small thing.
[pause 20]
