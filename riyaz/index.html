Basics of Hindustani music

Tap any note to hear it at your own Sa.

The seven swaras

Every melody is built from seven notes, the saptak: Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni. Sa is your home note. You choose where it sits for your voice, and every other note is measured from it. Sa and Pa never change. The other five each have a second form, which gives twelve notes in all.

Shuddha means natural. Komal (flat) forms exist for Re, Ga, Dha and Ni, written in small letters: r g d n. Tivra (sharp) exists only for Ma, written M♯.

Raga

A raga is more than a scale. It has rules for moving through the notes, and those rules give it a mood.

  • Aroha and avroha: the way up and the way down. They can differ.
  • Vadi and samvadi: the most important note and its partner.
  • Pakad: a short phrase that identifies the raga at once.
  • Samay: the time of day it is traditionally sung.

Good first ragas: Bhupali (five notes: S R G P D, evening) and Yaman (all seven with tivra Ma, early night). Both are in Practice.

Taal and laya

Taal is the repeating rhythm cycle, counted in matras (beats). The first beat is sam, where phrases resolve. Tali is a clap and khali a wave of the hand. Laya is tempo: vilambit (slow), madhya (medium), drut (fast).

Teentaal: 16 beats, 4 + 4 + 4 + 4

A 15-minute daily riyaz

  1. 3 min Sa with the tanpura. Hold it steady on "aa".
  2. 4 min Aroha and avroha, slowly, then long notes.
  3. 4 min One alankar. Raise the tempo only when it is clean.
  4. 4 min One raga piece or a song.

Your Sa

Sa is the home note every other note is measured from. Men usually sit near C3 to D3 (Safed 1, 2), women near G3 to A3 (Safed 5, 6).

Note length

How long a note sounds when you tap it: the swaras and octaves in Learn, Hear it, and the keys and strings in Instruments. A longer note gives you time to match it with your voice. Scales play each note for a quarter of this. Practice pieces have their own note length, set on the Practice page.
